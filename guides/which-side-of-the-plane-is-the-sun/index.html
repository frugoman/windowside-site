Which side of the plane is the sun on?

Published 2026-11-09

Sun in your eyes for five hours, or a perfect sunrise over the wing? The sun is on a predictable side of the plane. You can work it out before you pick a seat, from three things: the direction you fly, the local time and where you are on Earth.

The short rule (Northern Hemisphere, outside the tropics)

At local midday the sun is in the south. That gives the simple version:

  • Flying east: the sun is on the right side.
  • Flying west: the sun is on the left side.
  • Flying north: the sun is on the right in the morning (it is in the east) and on the left in the afternoon (it is in the west).
  • Flying south: the sun is on the left in the morning, on the right in the afternoon.

Flip this in the Southern Hemisphere, outside the tropics, where the midday sun is in the north: east means left, west means right.

For comfort, sit on the side away from the sun: left on eastbound daytime flights, right on westbound ones in the Northern Hemisphere. For the sunrise or sunset show, sit on the side with the sun.

The reason: the sun moves, and so does the plane

The sun rises in the east, is highest at midday and sets in the west. So the side depends on the time, not only on the heading. A flight east in the morning has the sun almost ahead of the plane, a little to the right; the same flight in the afternoon has it behind the plane on the right.

The sun's height matters too. A low sun, near sunrise and sunset, shines straight through the window. A high midday sun is mostly above the window line, which is why a long daytime flight is less bright than you expect once the sun climbs.

Why long flights are not simple

  • Great-circle routes curve. A flight from Europe to North America starts west-north-west and ends south-west. The heading changes by tens of degrees, and with it the side of the sun.
  • You fly across time zones. An eastbound overnight flight from the Americas to Europe meets a very short night, and the sunrise arrives early, ahead of the plane. Your body clock and the sun disagree, so the sun side at your "midnight" can be daylight outside.
  • Near the equator and the poles the rule weakens. Between the tropics the sun passes overhead, and which side it is on depends on the season. In polar summer, the sun does not set.
  • Season shifts it. In summer, the sun rises and sets north of due east and west in the Northern Hemisphere; in winter, south of them.

So treat the rule as a good start for flights under about five hours at mid-latitudes, and compute for longer ones.

How to compute it properly

For any flight you need the sun's position along the route at each time. That means the sun's azimuth (compass bearing) and its elevation (height) at the plane's location and time, compared with the plane's heading. If the sun's bearing minus the plane's heading is between 0 and 180 degrees, the sun is on the right; if it is between 180 and 360, it is on the left. Add the elevation: below about 10 degrees it shines straight in, above 60 it is mostly overhead.

You do not need to do this by hand. Windowside's pre-flight brief computes the sun's position along your great-circle route every five minutes, on the phone, from your departure time. Does the side matter if you can close the shade?

Less than it used to. The shade blocks glare, but the shade is yours only if you sit at the window. The person sitting next to the window controls it, and the cabin crew often ask to close all shades at night. Electronic dimmable windows (on the Boeing 787, for example) do not block light as fully as a pull-down shade. A seat on the shaded side avoids the whole argument and keeps the cabin cooler in the sun.

FAQ

Which side of the plane gets the sun when flying east?

In the Northern Hemisphere, outside the tropics, the sun is on the right side of the plane on an eastbound daytime flight, so the left side is shadier.

Which side of the plane gets the sun when flying west?

On the left, in the same conditions, so the right side is the shady side.

Does the sun side change during a flight?

It can. Long routes curve, you cross time zones, and the sun moves across the sky. Check the sun's position along the route, not only at departure.

Which side is best for sunrise?

The side where the sun appears, which depends on your heading and the time. An app that computes it along your route, like Windowside's pre-flight brief, will tell you.
