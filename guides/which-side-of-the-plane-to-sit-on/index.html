Which side of the plane to sit on for the best view

Published 2026-11-09

There is no "best side" of a plane. There is a best side for your flight, and you can work it out in two minutes if you know three things: which way you fly, where the interesting thing is, and which runway you will probably land on. This guide shows the method, then the exceptions that catch people out.

The one rule that decides everything

Face the direction the plane is flying. Left is to your left, right to your right. So the question is only: is the thing I want to see on the left or right of the flight path?

Do it with a compass bearing:

  1. Take the plane's heading, for example 270° for due west.
  2. Take the bearing from the plane to the landmark, for example 315° (north-west).
  3. Subtract: 315 − 270 = 45. A result between 0 and 180 means right; between 180 and 360 means left.

Heading west with a landmark to the north-west puts it on the right. If the landmark is south of your track, it is on the left. A feature that is dead ahead or behind is not on either side: it is only visible if the plane flies over it.

Shortcut for flights that go mainly east-west, in the Northern Hemisphere: north of the route is right when flying west and left when flying east. In the Southern Hemisphere the geometry is the same, only the landmarks change sides.

Why great-circle routes fool you

Long flights do not follow straight lines on a flat map. They follow the shortest path on a sphere, which looks like a curve towards the pole. A flight from Europe to the US east coast flies north-west first, then west, then south-west. The heading changes by tens of degrees, so a landmark can start on one side and end up on the other. For long-haul flights, ask where the plane will be when it passes the landmark, not where it takes off.

Air traffic control also moves you off the planned line: weather, traffic and the jet stream shift routes by tens of kilometres every day. For a landmark that is far off the track, the side is safe. For one that is close (a city under the flight path, a single peak), treat the side as a good bet, not a promise.

Takeoff and landing are where the views are

At cruise altitude, 10 to 12 km up, you see a lot of cloud and not much detail. The low part of the flight is the memorable one, and it depends on the runway in use:

  • Airports pick the runway from the wind, so the approach direction flips between days. The same flight may come in from the sea one day and from the land the next.
  • Planes land and take off into the wind, so the wind forecast for the airport tells you the likely direction. The airline cannot promise a runway in advance.
  • A city you want to see on landing is visible when the approach passes over or beside it. Check the airport's approach on a map and see which side the city is on for each direction of approach.

If both approaches are plausible, pick the side with the better view for the more common one, and keep your eyes open: a window seat on either side is a good seat.

Things you can use on almost any route

  • Coastlines: if the route runs along a coast, the side with the coast shows it. Work it out from the bearing rule: with the sea on your left, you want the left seat to see the shore line, the right seat to see the land behind it.
  • Big mountain ranges (the Alps, the Andes, the Pyrenees, the Rockies) are tall enough to see from 10 km, from a distance of 100 km or more. The side they are on follows the bearing rule above.
  • Sunrise and sunset are a different question: the side with the sun. We cover it in the guide to which side of the plane the sun is on.

Window seats that have no window

Many planes have a seat row where the window is misaligned with the seat, or a wall instead of a window. Check the seat map on SeatGuru or the airline's own plan before you pay for a seat. Rows over the wing have the wing in your view, so for photos pick a row ahead of the wing or well behind it.

FAQ

Is it better to sit on the left or right side of a plane?

Neither is better in general. It depends on your route. Work out whether the landmark you want is to the left or right of the flight path, then pick that side. For a route you do not know, use a map or an app that shows the track.

Which side is best for landing views?

It depends on the runway in use, which depends on the wind. Check the airport's usual approach direction and which side the city is on in that case. The side can change from day to day.

Does the side of the plane matter for sunrises?

Yes, but that is a separate question from landmarks. See the guide on which side of the plane the sun is on.
